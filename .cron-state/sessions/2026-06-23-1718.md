## Deferred (and why)

- **Hopper rule reorder-by-drag** — listed as a candidate since
  round 26; round-31's cascade UNDO further reduces its
  priority. A user who reorders wrong can now revert the whole
  cascade in N clicks; the drag affordance becomes a polish
  rather than a friction fix.
- **Undo ring popover** — round 31 surfaced a "Step N of M"
  counter chip; a future round could promote it to a clickable
  popover listing per-entry labels + timestamps for a user who
  wants to skip directly to a specific cascade depth rather
  than walking newest-first. Not shipped tonight because the
  one-line chip is the minimum information needed and the
  popover would add overlay-stack complexity for marginal value.
- **Drilldown row -> cross-surface filter** — clicking a
  fall-through filename in the popover would carry the search
  query into the document inspector. Listed as a candidate
  since round 27; round-31 picked undo-ring because it COMPOUNDS
  round-30 value and the drilldown surface didn't change tonight.

## Process notes

- Same 5-layer cadence as rounds 19-30 (backend primitive -> TS
  mirror -> Tauri command + TS wrapper -> bridge layer ->
  demo-able UI). Slice 151's bridge is structurally similar to
  round-30's slice-146 bridge but operates on the LIVE state
  (ReorderUndoEntry[]) while slice 149's mirror operates on the
  SUMMARY wire shape (UndoEntrySummary). The split lets audit
  consumers read the compact summary without needing the live
  snapshots, and lets the UI's $state stay in the live-ring
  shape without serialising snapshots over the wire.
- The newest-first selectActiveUndo walker is the load-bearing
  cascade primitive. Oldest-first would surface the oldest ready
  entry, breaking the Cmd-Z mental model ("undo what I just
  did"). Walking newest -> oldest means undoing pops the newest
  ready entry and the next-newest ready becomes active on the
  next render.
- One test scaffolding mistake caught in mid-build: I asserted
  `totalReady === 1` for a cascade where all 3 entries had
  snapshots of the same rule set, which means all 3 were
  technically ready against any live permutation. The assertion
  was wrong, not the code; fixed to `totalReady === 3` and
  added a comment explaining why.
