# Slab 3.31.0 — Atlas Lite

**Open Slab. Pick up exactly where you left off.**

Most PDF apps treat your home screen as a footnote. Adobe buries it under
five tabs. PDF Expert flattens every file into the same anonymous tile.
We rebuilt the moment you open Slab around a single idea: **momentum**.

## Continue reading

The first thing you see is a hero card for the document you were last
reading, with a thumbnail, a progress bar that pulses as it appears, and a
big resume button. One keystroke (**⌘0**) and you're back on the exact page
you closed on Friday afternoon.

Every recent file remembers its last position. Close mid-page, reopen
tomorrow — Slab lands you on the same page, with a quiet toast confirming
"Resumed at page 47 of 120" in case you want to start over.

## Pin what matters

Right-click any recent file (or hit the pin icon on hover) and it floats to
a dedicated **Pinned** strip at the top of your Home. Pinned files are
exempt from auto-eviction, so the contract you reference every day stays
one click away forever.

## Eight dots that mean something

Each card on Home shows eight progress dots. Half-filled = halfway through.
A glance tells you which document needs another sitting and which is
already in the rear-view mirror — no math, no spreadsheets.

## ⌘0 from anywhere

A new top-level **Home** action group puts two shortcuts in your hands:

- **⌘0** — Go to Recents Home from any document, any panel.
- **⌘⇧0** — Continue reading: jump directly into whatever you were last
  reading without a stopover at Home.

Every pinned and recent file is also a command-palette entry now — open
the palette (⌘K), type any filename fragment, hit Enter, you're in. No
file picker. No menu spelunking.

## All local

Your reading habits never leave your machine. Pin state, last-page
position, thumbnails — all `localStorage`, all on your disk. No telemetry,
no cloud sync, no "AI improving your experience" — just a tool that
remembers because remembering is its job.

That'll all change the day we ship the Pro tier with multi-device sync. It
will be opt-in, end-to-end encrypted, and priced at $49 one-time or $5/month.
Until then: free, local, private.
